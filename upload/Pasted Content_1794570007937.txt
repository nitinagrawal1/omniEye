3. Findings and decisions (read before building)
1.  Hotkey conflict. The mockups show Alt + Space, but the PRD describes clicking a floating icon, and Alt+Space is the Windows window-menu shortcut (also PowerToys Run’s default). Decision: show Alt + Space as in the mockups; ask the client to confirm the final hotkey.
2.  Latency claim needs care. The PRD defines the under-300 ms target as mouse release to image buffer complete, not time to the AI’s answer. The copy says “instant AI answers” next to “sub-300ms”. Decision: keep the client’s wording; add a small footnote under the hero: “Sub-300ms refers to screen-region capture. AI response time varies.” Client to approve. Investors will probe this.
3.  Platform overclaim. The About copy says OmniEye “works seamlessly across every major platform and desktop application”; the PRD’s MVP milestone is AutoCAD and Excel in four weeks, and the prototype script is a fixed-coordinate screenshot. Decision: keep verbatim, but badge the product “Private Beta” and flag for client review before investor use.
4.  Medical and HIPAA visuals. Mockups 4 and 5 show clinical insights, “potential diagnostic conflict” and a “HIPAA Privacy Mode” label. Do not claim HIPAA compliance or diagnostic capability anywhere on the site. If used, crop out the HIPAA chip and add the caption “Illustrative concept. OmniEye is not a medical device.”
5.  Mockup content is AI-generated and inconsistent. Small text is garbled in all UI mockups, and values contradict each other (HbA1c shown as 6.5, 6.8 and 7.2; “meets BOCA requirements” in the AutoCAD HUD). Decision: show mockups at sizes where garbling is not readable, label them “Product concept”, and offer the client cleaner re-renders later.
6.  Two versions of the logo. The product UI uses a line-art eye in a diamond with a blue-to-gold gradient. The supplied logo file (image 8) is a heavier filled navy/blue emblem with circuit lines on a white canvas, and its navy parts disappear on dark backgrounds. Decision: use the line-art mark for nav, favicon and HUD cards; use the full emblem only on a light badge or in the OG image. Request a transparent SVG of both from the client.
7.  Form fields differ. The questionnaire wants Name, Work Email and Role/Use-case; the copy shows one email field. Decision: build the three-field form; the questionnaire has higher precedence.
8.  Three CTA labels. “Join Early Access” (nav), “Request Beta Access” (hero), “Get Early Access” (footer submit). Keep all three exactly as written; they all lead to the same form.
9.  Slogans. SAS (See Analyze Show) is the client’s tagline. “See More. Work Smarter.” appears only inside storyboard image 6. Use SAS as the official tagline; use the other only inside that image.
10.  Internal note, not page copy. “Shiva Third Eye concept mark” is a design note. Do not mention it on the site.
11.  Do not name the AI vendor. The PRD and script use Gemini; the copy never names it. Keep vendor names, API payload limits and fallback thresholds off the page.
12.  Third-party trademarks. AutoCAD, Adobe Acrobat and Chrome appear in the mockups. Add to the footer: “Product names and logos are trademarks of their respective owners. No affiliation or endorsement implied.”
13.  Demo video does not exist yet. Build a polished placeholder plus a one-line config for the final video. Image 6 is a ready storyboard for the 60-second script (Section 7.3).
14.  Platforms in footer copy. The CTA subtext promises Windows and macOS. Confirm with the client that macOS is still on the roadmap.

4.3 Problem (#problem)
• Heading: Stop Copying, Pasting, and Uploading Screenshots.
• Body: Modern knowledge workers—from engineers reviewing complex CAD drawings to analysts reading dense reports—lose hours manually feeding context into AI chatbots. Switching tabs breaks focus and slows down execution.
• Visual idea: a muted “before” strip (copy → switch tab → paste → upload → wait) that collapses into a single glowing lasso gesture. Pure CSS or SVG.
4.4 How OmniEye Works (#how-it-works) — 3-step grid
1.  Trigger Anywhere. Press a single global hotkey to summon the translucent OmniEye overlay over any active software—from AutoCAD and PDFs to browsers and spreadsheets.
2.  Lasso Your Context. Drag a quick selection around any diagram, table, code block, or text. Our sub-300ms OCR and vision engine captures and stitches the exact visual context.
3.  Act Instantly. Ask a question, extract data, or run a custom prompt right inside the floating HUD without ever leaving your workspace.
Visual: each card gets a mini illustration: step 1 uses the Alt + Space pill (image 1), step 2 uses the animated lasso (Section 7.1), step 3 uses the HUD answer card (image 7 or image 6 panel 4).
4.5 Built for Speed & Privacy (#features) — feature showcase
• Ultra-Low Latency (<300ms). Engineered for instant capture and context stitching so you stay in flow.
• Featherlight Footprint (<2% CPU). Runs smoothly in the background alongside heavy desktop applications without slowing down your machine.
• Privacy-First Whitelisting. You control which apps OmniEye can see. Sensitive windows stay completely invisible by default.
Visual: three feature cards with a large privacy panel beside them (image 2: AutoCAD 2026 ON/Visible, Adobe Acrobat PDF ON, Google Chrome ON, Banking & Finance App Hidden by Default/OFF, Personal Messages locked/OFF; footer line “Privacy-First Engine: Sensitive windows remain invisible • <2% CPU”).

5. Visual identity and layout direction
Concept: the Drawing Sheet. OmniEye’s users live in CAD drawings, spreadsheets and reports, so the site is built like an engineer’s drawing sheet seen through a camera viewfinder: crop marks, hairline dimension lines, sheet numbers and a title-block footer. The tagline gives the page its structure: See (hero, problem), Analyze (how it works, features), Show (industries, about, sign-up). This deliberately replaces the standard dark-SaaS look: centered gradient headline, two equal columns, three identical glass cards, blue-purple glow.
Palette: Ink, Bone and Aureate. The client’s logo is navy, blue and gold. All three stay, but the hierarchy flips: gold leads, navy gives depth, and blue becomes a single-purpose signal color. Confirm with the client in one line.

4.  How it works, “scrollytelling stage”: no cards. One sticky stage (the AutoCAD view, built live) changes as the visitor scrolls past three large numbered steps in serif numerals: the Alt + Space pill appears, the sapphire lasso draws, the HUD answer lands. On mobile the stage is a shorter sticky strip above each step.
5.  Features, “spec sheet”: giant numerals are the design: under 300ms, under 2% CPU, and “Hidden by default”, laid out as a ruled title-block table with the feature copy under each. 7.  Industries, “index list”: three huge serif rows (01 Education & Research, 02 Healthcare & Diagnostics, 03 IT & Software Engineering). Hover, focus or tap expands a row to show its copy and a mockup crop in a sticky preview. Built as an accessible accordion.
8.  Final CTA, “gold sheet”: full-bleed aureate background with ink text, the page’s climax and the only gold-filled area. The form looks like a title block: large underline inputs, ink button with bone label.
9.  Footer, “title block”: ruled cells for Project, Sheet, Scale, Rev, plus © 2026 OmniEye. Dubai, UAE. and the Privacy Policy link; trademark line beneath. Sheet numbers and scale labels are decoration only, never copy that claims anything.
Mockup treatment. The supplied mockups use neon cyan glows and garbled small text. Use only the clean AutoCAD base screens (images 3 and 7) and rebuild the lasso rectangle, HUD answer card, hotkey pill and latency chips live in HTML/SVG in the palette above. Any other image used as is (images 4 and 5) gets desaturated and toned toward ink and bone, with no glow.
Never do: centered hero with gradient headline; two equal columns of text plus a floating card; three identical rounded cards; glassmorphism blur panels; neon glows or mesh gradients; icon-in-a-circle feature rows; sparkle or emoji icons; fake testimonials, logo walls or invented stats.

7. Interactions and motion
All motion is subtle, CSS-first, animates only transform and opacity, never transition: all, and fully respects prefers-reduced-motion (static state instead).
7.1 Animated lasso demo (How it works, step 2)
Built live in HTML/SVG over the AutoCAD base image, not as a pre-rendered GIF, and used in the Section 5 scrollytelling stage. A cursor glides in, drags a dashed sapphire rectangle (scale transform, not width/height), the selected region stays bright while the rest dims, then a mono chip “Lasso Context Capture · 240ms” and the HUD answer card slide in. Runs once as the step scrolls into view, with a visible Replay control; reduced-motion users get the final state.
7.2 Page effects
Restrained and purposeful: headings and numerals rise and fade in once; strike-through lines draw across words; hairline dimension lines draw in (stroke-dashoffset); buttons shift 2 px on hover with stronger contrast; one small glow only on the active lasso or selected state. No parallax, no particles, no auto-moving backgrounds. Smooth anchor scrolling with scroll-margin-top for the sticky header.
7.3 Demo video modal
• Opens from the hero button and the video box. Accessible dialog: focus trap, Esc closes, focus returns to the trigger, overscroll-behavior: contain, background scroll locked.
• One config value, DEMO_VIDEO_URL. If set to a YouTube/Vimeo (use the privacy-enhanced domain) or MP4 link, it plays on demand with no autoplay. If empty, show the placeholder: a clean 16:9 frame with the poster, a “Demo video coming soon” message and a button to the waitlist form.
• Script reference for the real video (from image 6): 0:00–0:10 open AutoCAD and a PDF report side by side; 0:10–0:25 press Alt + Space, overlay appears with the third-eye icon, “See More. Work Smarter.”; 0:25–0:45 drag around the beam schedule, scanning indicator; 0:45–1:00 HUD shows extracted beam details (type B-2, 250 × 500 mm, 4Ø14 top + 2Ø10 bottom, stirrups Ø8 @ 150 c/c), key insights, related sheets and a copyable answer.

Behavior:
• Never block paste or typing; validate on submit with inline errors beside the field and focus the first error. Submit stays enabled until the request starts, then shows a spinner and keeps its label.
• Hidden honeypot field plus basic rate limiting; no CAPTCHA at this stage.
• States: idle, submitting, success (inline confirmation, aria-live=polite, e.g. “You’re on the list. We’ll email you when your beta invite is ready.”), duplicate email (friendly message, no error tone), and failure with a retry button and a fallback “email us” link. No dead ends.
• Small line under the button: “By joining you agree to our Privacy Policy.” (link). No marketing pop-ups or exit-intent modals.
Integration (required by client):
• Form posts to a serverless route (/api/waitlist) which forwards to a Google Apps Script web app that appends a row to a Google Sheet (timestamp, name, email, role, page URL, UTM parameters) and sends an instant notification email to the client’s inbox. This avoids the opaque-response and CORS problems of posting straight to Apps Script from the browser.
• Environment variables: WAITLIST_WEBHOOK_URL, WAITLIST_SECRET, NOTIFY_EMAIL. Fallback option if the client prefers: Formspree or Web3Forms.
• Deliver the Apps Script code and a three-step setup note in the README.
Privacy Policy page (/privacy): short, plain-language placeholder covering what is collected (name, work email, role), why (beta invitations and product updates), where it is stored (Google Workspace), no sale of data, how to request deletion, contact email. Mark clearly for the client’s legal review before launch; mention applicable UAE data protection law.
9. Technical, performance and SEO requirements
• Speed target: under 2 seconds to meaningful content on a mid-range phone over 4G; Lighthouse 90+ on Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO. Static output, no heavy animation libraries, fonts subset and preloaded, third-party scripts avoided, video loaded only on demand.
• Responsive: verified at 360, 390, 768, 1280 and ultra-wide widths; respect safe areas; never disable pinch zoom; hit targets at least 44 px on mobile; mobile inputs at 16 px or larger to prevent iOS zoom; no horizontal scrollbars.
• SEO: title “OmniEye — See Analyze Show | Intent-Aware AI Desktop Overlay”; meta description “OmniEye is an intent-aware AI desktop overlay. Lasso any screen region and get instant AI answers in under 300ms—without leaving your workflow. Join the beta.” ; canonical URL from an environment variable (domain pending); Open Graph and Twitter summary_large_image tags; OG image 1200 × 630 (dark background, logo, headline, SAS tagline, blue-gold glow) so investor link previews look right; favicon plus apple-touch icon from the eye mark; theme-color matching the background; color-scheme: dark; sitemap and robots; JSON-LD SoftwareApplication (Windows, macOS; no price).
• Analytics: none by default; privacy-friendly option (Plausible or Vercel Analytics) can be switched on if the client wants visit counts for investors.
• Hosting: deploy to the client’s Vercel, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages account; connect getomnieye.com or omnieye.app when DNS access arrives; HTTPS and www-to-apex redirect.
• Ownership: full source in a Git repository transferred to the client, with README (setup, env vars, deploy, how to swap the demo video and edit copy).
10. Quality checklist (from the supplied guidelines)
Interaction and accessibility
• Full keyboard support; visible :focus-visible rings that are never hidden by the sticky header; skip-to-content link; one h1, ordered h2–h3.
• Native elements (button, a, label, select) before ARIA; icon-only buttons have aria-label; decorative graphics aria-hidden; status never conveyed by color alone.
• Contrast checked (APCA-minded) on every muted text and glow-on-dark combination; hover, active and focus states increase contrast.
• Links use real <a> tags; anchors support middle-click; URL hash reflects the section.
Performance and polish
• Explicit image dimensions, preloaded hero image, lazy-loaded rest, no layout shift; skeleton or reserved space for the video frame.
• touch-action: manipulation; no outline: none without a replacement; tabular numbers for latency and spec figures.
• Layered shadows, crisp translucent borders, nested border radii (child no larger than parent).
AI-product UX pitfalls to avoid
• Clear button labels and microcopy; no mystery icons; no pop-ups or dark patterns.
• Every state designed: loading, empty, error, success, offline.
• Honest claims: no unverified compliance badges, no implied availability beyond private beta (see Section 3).
• Mobile treated as a primary experience, not an afterthought.
